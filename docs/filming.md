# Filming for analysis

The analyzer sees your body as 33 points. It can count what it can see, and it times what it can't count. A good camera position does more for accuracy than anything in the code.

**Do**
- **Prop the phone** on a plate, box or tripod, and don't touch it until the workout is over.
- **Landscape** (sideways). If you film portrait or the phone records sideways, it's rotated automatically, but landscape fits more of the gym.
- **Fit every station**: barbell, pull-up rig, rope area, machines. If you walk out of frame, that time can only be counted as a transition.
- **Film side-on or at 45°** to the rig, so your body stays visible above the bar or rings. Filmed from behind the rig, muscle-ups and pull-ups get hidden by its uprights.
- **Start recording before the clock starts**, with the timer audible. The beep helps line up the start.
- **One athlete in frame.** With a partner, film from an angle where their station is out of shot.

**Avoid**
- A phone so close that you leave the frame overhead or on the floor.
- Bright windows behind you: silhouettes track worse.
- Mirrors in frame: reflections can be tracked as a second person.

**What's hard by nature**
- Double-under counts are within about ±10%. Time and breaks are reliable.
- Calories and distances can't be seen. They're taken from the workout, and only the time is measured.
- Muscle-ups and kipping movements partly behind a rig are timed but not counted. The report says when that happened.
